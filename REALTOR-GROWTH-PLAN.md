## PHASE 1 — Finish licensing (next 2–6 weeks)

Idaho requires, in order:

1. **90 hours pre-licensing** (two 45-hour courses) from an approved provider — you're doing this via The CE Shop. Finish both module exams. *You have 1 year from course start to use it.*
2. **Fingerprints + FBI/state background check** (Idaho Code 54-2012 "honesty & integrity"). Book at `pearsonwest.ibtfingerprint.com` or IdentoGO/Idemia (866) 761-8069. **~$61.25.** Takes 1–12 weeks and results are only good for **6 months** — so do this NOW, in parallel with exam prep, not after.
3. **State licensing exam via Pearson VUE** — national + state portions, **70%** to pass each, **~$80**. You can fail/retake just one portion. Must pass both within 1 year of finishing coursework.
4. **License application to IREC** — log into your **DOPL Online Services** account, **~$160** salesperson fee. Submit after passing the exam + fingerprints clear.
5. **E&O insurance** — required before your license goes *active*. Many brokerages (incl. KW) offer a group policy; confirm cost before you commit.

## PHASE 3 — Activate your license & become a REALTOR®

A "real estate licensee" ≠ a "Realtor." To get MLS access and the Realtor title:

1. **Sponsoring broker adds you to their IREC roster** (this activates your license).
2. **Join Boise Regional REALTORS® (BRR)** — your local association. This auto-enrolls you in **Idaho REALTORS®** and **NAR** (National Association of REALTORS). Budget ~$1,000–$1,500 in first-year dues/setup.
3. **Intermountain MLS (IMLS)** subscription — your access to listings + comps. Monthly/quarterly fee.
4. **SentriLock lockbox + Supra/SentriKey app** — to show homes (your board will set this up).
5. **Set up your tools:** brokerage CRM (KW Command, or your own), e-signature (Authentisign/Dotloop/DocuSign), forms (zipForm/Idaho REALTORS forms library).

**Important 2026 context:** Post-NAR-settlement, **buyer-broker agreements are now required before showing homes**, and commissions are explicitly negotiable and not assumed-paid by the seller's side. Learn the new buyer-rep workflow cold — it's where new agents get tripped up right now.

## PHASE 5 — Build your lead-generation engine

New agents fail from **no leads**, not lack of knowledge. Run multiple channels:

**1. Sphere of Influence (SOI) — your #1 source year one**
- List every person you know (phone contacts, social, former coworkers, the clinical/Idaho Academy network, the business owners you've emailed).
- Announce your new career to all of them (call/text/email + social post).
- Add them all to your CRM and touch base every 30–60 days.
- *As a newcomer your SOI is thin — so over-invest in the next channels.*

**2. Geographic farming**
- Pick ONE neighborhood (300–500 homes) where you'd like to specialize.
- Become the local expert: monthly market postcards/emails, a neighborhood landing page (you can build it), door-knocking, sponsor a community event.

**3. Online / your digital edge**
- SEO + Google Business Profile (Phase 4) — rank for local searches.
- Run targeted Facebook/Instagram lead ads (you understand ad funnels).
- Capture leads with a home-valuation tool and a "Boise relocation guide" (you literally relocated — write the guide you wish you'd had).

**4. Open houses** — the fastest way for a new agent to meet ready buyers. Ask your brokerage to let you host other agents' listings.

**5. Referral partners** — lenders, title reps, the RE photographers/drone/PM contacts in your inbox, contractors. Trade referrals.

**6. Niche: relocation buyers** — you just moved here. You can authentically serve people moving to Idaho from out of state (a huge Treasure Valley segment).

## PHASE 7 — Money & runway (don't skip this)

Real estate income is lumpy and back-loaded. New agents quit because they run out of cash, not motivation.

- **Commission math (Treasure Valley):** ~$500K median sale × ~2.5% buy-side = ~$12,500 gross → minus brokerage split (e.g., 30%) → ~$8,750 → minus taxes/expenses. Plan on **3–4 closings before you see steady income**, and closings lag the contract by 30–45 days.
- **Cash runway:** have **6 months of living expenses** saved, or keep a **bridge income** (your web-design business and/or the clinical shifts via Idaho Academy) going during ramp-up.
- **Set up the business properly:** consider an **LLC** for your real estate activity (check Idaho rules — many agents operate as sole props initially), a **separate business bank account**, and **set aside ~25–30% of every commission for taxes** (you're now self-employed — quarterly estimated taxes apply).
- **Track expenses** from Day 1: dues, MLS, E&O, marketing, mileage, software. All deductible.
- **Startup budget (first 6 months):** plan ~$2,000–$4,000 (licensing + dues + MLS + E&O + marketing). Your web skills cut the marketing/website portion dramatically.

## PHASE 8 — 12-month growth & beyond

- **Months 1–3:** licensed, joined brokerage, lead-gen engine live, first deal in progress.
- **Months 4–6:** 1–3 closings, refine your niche (relocation? a specific neighborhood?), collect reviews/testimonials.
- **Months 7–12:** consistent pipeline (aim for 1+ closing/month), build a referral database, ask for repeat/referral business systematically.
- **Year 2+:** consider designations (ABR – Accredited Buyer's Rep, SRS, or a relocation cert), build a sphere database of 200+, and eventually a **team or productized lead-gen** (your digital marketing skill scales here — you could run lead-gen for other agents too).
- **Stretch:** after 2 years actively licensed, you qualify to pursue an **Idaho broker license** (more education + experience required) for higher splits and the ability to run your own shop.
